# Neutral Microcopy

## Problem

Clinical jargon, accusatory language, or stigmatizing terminology in UI copy increases distress and erodes trust. It also creates barriers for non-technical stakeholders reading reports. Labels that pathologize, sensationalize, or imply fault shape how users understand the material they are reviewing and how they understand their own performance.

## Design Guidance

Use plain, neutral, non-judgmental language throughout. Avoid terms that imply user fault or failure. Use "you can" over "you must" where policy allows. Use factual, descriptive labels for sensitive content categories rather than clinical diagnoses or sensational descriptions.

Write for all audiences: examiners, investigators, legal reviewers, managers, and court. A report section that can only be understood by the examiner who created it has not been written for the context it will actually be used in.

Language should normalize the difficulty of the work without dramatizing it. "It is common to feel unsettled when reviewing this material" is neutral. "Warning: This content is extremely disturbing" is not neutral; it adds anticipatory stress before the user has seen anything.

## Do

- Error messages: "Let's try that again." "This step needs your signature before continuing."
- Category labels: descriptive and factual ("Explicit imagery," "Violence," "Communications involving a minor")
- "You can return to this step at any time."
- Neutral prompts: "How would you like to proceed?" over "You must choose one of the following."
- Wellbeing copy: "It is common to feel unsettled reviewing this material. Resources are available in the sidebar."

## Don't

- Error messages that imply fault: "Invalid input," "You failed to complete this step," "Incorrect"
- Sensational category labels: "Horrific content," "Extreme violence"
- Clinical diagnoses as UI labels: "PTSD-triggering content"
- System messages that dramatize content before the user has seen it
- "You must" when the policy allows "you can"

## Risk Level

**Medium** - Failure increases distress through language and creates barriers for non-technical audiences.

## Principles

- Safety
- Cultural, Historical, and Gender Issues
- Empowerment, Voice, and Choice
