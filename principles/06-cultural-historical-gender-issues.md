# Principle 06: Cultural, Historical, and Gender Issues

## Definition

The product respects diverse identities and does not make assumptions about gender, relationship status, cultural background, or prior experience with trauma or investigation. Language, imagery, and identity fields are inclusive by default.

## In Digital Product Design

High-stakes and investigative tools are used by a diverse workforce and process data about diverse populations. The design should not encode assumptions about who the users are or who the subjects of investigations are.

This applies at every layer of the product. Category labels for sensitive content should be factual and descriptive, not sensational or clinical. Identity fields for subjects and users should support chosen names, pronouns, and non-binary options. Examples and imagery in documentation should be culturally neutral.

Cultural sensitivity also applies to how the product handles context. An investigation into domestic violence, a hate crime, a cybersecurity incident, and a financial fraud case each carry different cultural and historical weight. The product should not flatten those differences through generic copy or one-size-fits-all workflows.

Historical sensitivity means recognizing that some communities have experienced harm at the hands of law enforcement and investigative systems. A product that communicates with accusatory language or treats all users and subjects as adversaries by default compounds that harm.

## What Cultural, Historical, and Gender Issues Addresses

- Binary or restrictive identity fields
- Clinical or stigmatizing language in category labels and system messages
- Culturally biased examples, imagery, or defaults
- One-size-fits-all workflows that do not account for case context
