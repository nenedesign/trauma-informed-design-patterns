# Principle 02: Trustworthiness and Transparency

## Definition

The product behaves predictably and explains itself. Users understand what the system does, what it cannot do, who can see their work, and why certain steps are required. Actions have visible consequences before they are taken.

## In Digital Product Design

In forensic and investigative contexts, transparency is both a design principle and a legal requirement. Chain of custody, audit trails, and explainability of AI outputs are not optional features. They are conditions for the product to be used in evidence-based work at all.

Transparency means the system does not hide its actions. Every change to evidence, every access to case data, and every AI-generated output is attributable, traceable, and visible. A non-technical stakeholder reviewing a report should be able to understand how evidence was collected, processed, and interpreted, without needing to ask an examiner.

Trustworthiness means the product does what it says it does. It does not perform background actions users are unaware of. It does not produce outputs users cannot evaluate.

## What Trustworthiness and Transparency Addresses

- Hidden system actions or consequences
- AI outputs without explanation or source citation
- Chain-of-custody gaps in evidence handling
- Unclear data access, retention, or export policies
- Role-based permissions that are invisible at the point of use
