# Principle 05: Empowerment, Voice, and Choice

## Definition

Users have meaningful control over their experience. They can decide how deeply to engage with sensitive material, at what pace, and in what order. The product gives users agency over their interaction with the content, not only with the interface.

## In Digital Product Design

Empowerment is not about making everything optional. Some steps in a forensic workflow are required by law or policy. Empowerment means that within those constraints, users control the depth, pace, and sequence of their work.

A user who can expand or collapse graphic details, toggle image previews, set their own review mode, and save their progress at any point is empowered. A user who is forced through a fixed sequence, auto-exposed to sensitive content, and has no way to indicate their own capacity is not.

Voice means users can provide feedback that changes the product. This includes flagging, annotating, requesting support, and providing feedback on AI outputs. Users who interact with sensitive evidence daily have insight into what works and what causes harm. The product should have mechanisms to capture that.

Choice means the product does not funnel users into a single path when alternatives are possible. Display options (dark mode, font size, reduced motion), configurable workspaces, and review modes are not accessibility features only. They are design decisions that respect the diversity of how people do their best work.

## What Empowerment, Voice, and Choice Addresses

- Auto-exposure to sensitive content without user consent
- Fixed workflows with no ability to adjust pace, depth, or sequence
- No mechanism for users to flag distressing content or request support
- Display options buried in settings or absent entirely
